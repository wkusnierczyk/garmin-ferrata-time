Through the climb, each opened on the climber's section

To look at

  • The page opens on the section the climber is in; the chevrons step one section back or on, the left one dimmed on the first section and the right one on the last. Moving the climber does not change the selection; the button goes back to the climber's section.
  • The line above: the altitude at the section's foot and at its top, "1380 to 1490", "to" and the unit in grey, the whole centred. A flat section reads the same altitude twice.
  • The line below: the section's name, cut with an ellipsis if too long, followed by the symbols, standing on the name's baseline (not the bottom of its letters: a descender such as the y in "gully" does not move them; on the watch, the font's ascent gives the baseline), of the points it holds (danger, exit, rest, top: yellow triangle, green arrow, blue pause, red square), the whole centred. A section shows the points it includes or is next to: a point in it or at its foot or top, and a rest that is the section itself or comes right before or after it.
  • The gauge shows the selection: page 6b's gauge (by ascent) with the selected section at full colour and all the others dimmed, and no climber line. Its points sit inside the gauge, 4 px clear of the sections, so the gauge holds only the sections; they are at full colour where the selected section shows their symbol (below), dimmed elsewhere. Each section gets at least 3° of the sweep (the menu: none, 2, 3 or 5°), so a flat section such as a rest has an arc of its own to light up.
  • The flat ascent at the bottom shows the climber, as on page 6b.
