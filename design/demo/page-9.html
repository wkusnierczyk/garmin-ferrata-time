To look at

  • Section 5 is a stand-in descent (owner, 2026-11-23): the route climbs there; the mock turns it into a descent of its own height, to show how the pages draw one. Every altitude, the profile, the front view and the ascent's ring and numbers follow the real height (down there, and the next section starts lower); the stacks, the gauge by ascent's arcs and the climbed totals count height gained (flat there).
  • The page opens on the section the climber is in; the chevrons step one section back or on, the left one dimmed on the first section and the right one on the last. Moving the climber does not change the selection; the button goes back to the climber's section.
  • Above the box, the altitude at the section's top; below it, the altitude at its foot; each number with its unit centred on the screen together (owner, 2026-11-23; the numbers were right-aligned with the box's right edge). A flat section reads the same altitude twice.
  • Under the foot's altitude, the points the section holds as words (DANGER, EXIT, REST, TOP), each in its colour, joined by a dim dot, centred. A section shows the points it includes or is next to: a point in it or at its foot or top, and a rest that is the section itself or comes right before or after it.
  • The gauge shows the selection: page 6b's gauge (by ascent) with the selected section at full colour and all the others dimmed, and no climber line. Its points sit inside the gauge, 4 px clear of the sections, so the gauge holds only the sections; they are at full colour where the selected section shows their word (below), dimmed elsewhere. A flat section (a rest) has no arc by ascent: its blue mark lights up instead. Colours: danger yellow, exit green, rest blue; the top is the ascent's red dot at the gauge's end.
  • The points on the gauge (owner, 2026-11-23: the rings were small and hard to read on the watch) are short arc segments along the gauge's inside, 8 px thick and 24 px long with round ends (two thirds and twice the gauge's pen). A menu tries filled disks (18 px across) and the rings as they were. Segments and disks keep 4 px from each other: one that would touch another moves one lane further in, keeping its angle (on the demo route, the rest on the Ledge and the rockfall danger are 1° apart, and the exit at the Band and the rest on the Bench 5°).
