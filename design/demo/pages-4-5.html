Ferrata · pages 4 and 5, version 1

The difficulty column again, now to scale, in two variants: on page 4 each section's box is as tall as the metres it climbs; on page 5, as tall as its length along the route, with the ascent as a gauge around the edge. In the app the box heights would be a setting. The window moves with the climber, who stays a quarter of the way up from its bottom. Altitude ticks on the left, the sections' names on the right. The current box is filled, the others outlined; those behind the climber dimmed.

To look at

  • The window is 288 px tall on the 416 px screen. At 3 px per metre (the default) it shows 96 m: 24 m below the climber and 72 m above.
  • Flat sections (the Ledge and the Bench rests) climb nothing, so they have no height: each is a line across the column at its altitude, with its name.
  • Letters are centred on the part of a box that is in view, and left out when that part is too short for them. Names are centred the same way and cut with an ellipsis to fit the round screen.
  • On page 5 the ascent is a gauge: a thin arc around the edge from the start at the lower left, over the top, to the top of the route at the lower right, with the climber's orange ring at the height climbed. The metres climbed sit just past the start end, the metres left just past the top end, in the gap at the bottom. The stack is centred as a group (tick labels, column, 80 px for names); names stop inside the gauge.
  • By path, flat sections are real boxes, and walls are short. Altitude ticks sit where the route first reaches each altitude: they crowd on walls and spread on walks; a label is left out when it would touch the one below.
  • The boxes meet with no gap. A border between two boxes is drawn in the colour of the one closer to the current box; next to the current box, in the current box's colour.
  • The climber is the orange ring of the ascent widget on the column's edge, left by default or right (the menu chooses); no line across the column, which hid the letters. Optionally (off by default) a tick from the ring across the current box to the other edge: in the climber's orange, or black where the box is itself near that orange (K4 in the amber map); over or near the box's letters it stops short of them on both sides, passing under them.
  • The demo plays at a vertical pace: walks pass quickly, walls slowly, and each grade above walking 10 % slower than the one below. A flat section takes as long as 5 m of climbing, and the climber stops for 3 s at each rest point (the middle of the rest), at any speed.
  • Ticks run only along the route, not below its start or above its top. Ticks and labels are in the altitude unit: every 10 m by default, or every 10 ft in feet, which is dense; the step menu changes it. Resolution to be discussed.
  • On pages 4 and 5 the label of the next altitude to reach (on page 5 the first tick above the altitude now, which may sit further along the route than the climber), is larger than the others (100, 120, 150 or 200 % of their 18 px; the menu chooses, 200 % by default), and always shown; the labels just above and below it are larger too (150 % by default); the other labels make room around them.
  • The points of interest (on by default) as rings on the column's edge, as the climber's ring is, on the edge opposite it (the right, with the climber on the left), at their place on the column; in the colours of the other pages: four kinds, rests blue at the middle of the rest, exits green, dangers yellow, and the route's top red; dimmed once passed. The names move right a little to clear them. A point's name (on by default) beside its ring, in its colour, from one tick step before the climber reaches it to half a step after leaving it (page 4 at 20 m ticks: from 20 m below to 10 m above, by height; page 5: the same in metres of path): the kind of point, "Rest", "Exit", "Danger" or "Top" (a rest's section keeps its own name, such as Bench, as the white name). Points whose rings would touch show one ring and one name: of those in the climber's range, the first of danger, exit, rest, top; with none in range, the nearest to the climber. A section name a point's name would touch gives way.
  • The current section's letters, right of the column, are as large as the next altitude's label on the left: 52 px, Bold (owner, 2026-11-23; they were 36); the others' letters stay 28.
  • Not yet: a title above the stack, or the current difficulty and altitude below it, as in the sketch.
